UMTYMP Test Prep: goals and what students learn

A practice app for the quantitative-comparison section of the UMTYMP qualifying exam. It teaches students to think about math instead of grinding through arithmetic, and to do it fast.

Exam format assumed here (50 questions in 25 minutes) still needs to be confirmed against official UMTYMP sources.

2. What a question looks like

Each question shows two quantities. The student picks one of four answers.

Quantity Ax²
Quantity Bx

Condition: x ≠ 0

AQuantity A is greater
BQuantity B is greater
CThe two are equal
DCannot be determined

Shortcut: try x = 2 (A is bigger), then x = ½ (B is bigger). Two different results, so the answer is D. No calculation needed, about 15 seconds.

Many students answer A here because they forget fractions. That is the "fraction between 0 and 1" trap.

3. The coaching loop

  1. Practice: the student takes a mock exam or a short drill.
  2. Measure: the app records the time per question, the topic, which traps caught them, and whether they slowed down near the end.
  3. Coach: the results screen says three things: one real strength, the biggest bottleneck, and one habit to fix it. Every claim has a number behind it.
  4. Drill: a 10-question drill on exactly that weak spot. Then the loop starts again.

Example: "Exponents: 18 s average, 9/10 correct. Inequalities are costing you 45 s each. Always test x = 2, 0 and −1. Here's a 10-question drill."

4. What the student learns, in detail

Reasoning habits

  • Test cases first: try 2, 0, −1 and ½ before answering. If two cases disagree, the answer is D.
  • Compare, don't compute: cancel what both sides share, estimate sizes, skip exact values.
  • Read the conditions: "x ≠ 0", "x is an integer" and "not drawn to scale" change the answer.
  • Proof thinking: "always bigger" needs a reason; "it depends" needs two examples that disagree.

Number sense

  • Squaring a number between 0 and 1 makes it smaller.
  • An even power hides the sign: (−3)² = 3².
  • √ always means the non-negative root.
  • Zero and negative numbers break "obvious" patterns.

Exam skills

  • Pacing: a feel for 30 seconds per question and when to move on.
  • Calm under the clock: the timed format becomes familiar.
  • Stamina: staying accurate in the last 10 minutes, not just the first 10.
  • Smart skipping: when to skip, when to guess, and not over-using D.

Self-awareness

  • Sees real strengths and weak spots per topic, with numbers.
  • Fixes one bottleneck at a time instead of "study more".
  • Builds confidence: feedback names the habit to change, never "you're slow".

5. For parents

Parents see honest progress by topic. The app works offline with no account. Cloud sync is optional, set up by a parent, and stores nothing about the child except a display name. No ads, no trackers.
